I have published Review 2 of my Cochrane-style clinical AI meta-analysis series: fabricated and inaccurate citations generated by large language models for biomedical topics.

We screened 15,985 records and included 25 generation experiments (69 model arms) in which researchers asked a model for biomedical references and then checked every reference against PubMed, Crossref, Google Scholar or a publisher record. One published literature audit was kept in a separate stratum and never pooled with the experiments.

Headline results, all at very low certainty and with the reasons stated in the paper:
- 18.6% of generated references were completely fabricated (51 arms, 12,828 references, 95% CI 12.8 to 26.3)
- 29.4% were fully accurate on every field checked (42 arms, 12,002 references)
- Arms with web retrieval or browsing on pooled 5.0% fabricated, against 25.1% with retrieval off, on only five retrieval arms, so that gap is a signal to test, not a settled result
- No included study sat at low risk of bias on the adapted tool we specified before extraction; 15 of 25 sat at high risk, mostly because verification conduct was never reported

The spread between setups was the real finding. In one radiology study, six models given the same task fabricated between 3% and 61% of 480 references each. A pooled average describes that literature. It does not describe any one product.

Everything is open: manuscript, protocol, search log, screening and extraction data, risk of bias assessments, analysis code and forest plots, CC BY 4.0.

Title: One in five does not exist: a systematic review and meta-analysis of fabricated citations in LLM generated biomedical reference lists

Background: Health sciences librarians increasingly field reference lists drafted by large language models. No pooled estimate of fabrication in generated biomedical references existed.
Objectives: To pool, from generation experiments, the proportions of generated references that are completely fabricated, that carry any major inaccuracy, and that are fully accurate.
Methods: Cochrane Handbook 6.5 methods in structure, PRISMA 2020 reporting, dual LLM screening with coded adjudication under a single accountable human author, an adapted risk of bias tool specified before extraction, random effects meta-analysis of proportions (logit, REML), GRADE adapted for proportion evidence.
Results: 15,985 records screened; 25 experiments (69 model arms) included. Completely fabricated: pooled 18.6% (95% CI 12.8 to 26.3; 51 arms; I squared 97.7%). Fully accurate: 29.4% (19.5 to 41.7; 42 arms). Retrieval active arms pooled 5.0% fabricated against 25.1% with retrieval off (5 retrieval arms). All critical outcomes rated very low certainty, driven by unreported verification conduct and heterogeneity.
Conclusions: Generated reference lists need entry by entry verification against a database before use. The verification step is library work, and this literature rarely reports how it was done.
