## LinkedIn draft (for the parent agent to post per the author's updated rule)

I have published a corrected version of Review 2 of my Cochrane-style clinical AI meta-analysis series: fabricated and inaccurate citations generated by large language models for biomedical topics.

The first version screened 15,985 records and included 25 generation experiments. A robustness pass then re-adjudicated every screening disagreement by reading, chased citations systematically, extracted every study a second time independently, and settled every extraction discrepancy against the source text. The corrected version includes 63 generation experiments (148 model arms) in which researchers asked a model for biomedical references and then checked every reference against PubMed, Crossref, Google Scholar or a publisher record. One published literature audit was kept in a separate stratum and never pooled with the experiments.

Headline results, all at very low certainty and with the reasons stated in the paper:
- 19.4% of generated references were completely fabricated (113 arms, 22,461 references, 95% CI 14.9 to 24.8)
- 37.0% were fully accurate on every field checked (58 arms, 14,224 references)
- Arms with web retrieval or browsing on pooled 5.8% fabricated, against 31.0% with retrieval off, on 26 retrieval arms, so that gap is firmer than in the first version and still tangled with model generation
- No included study sat at low risk of bias on the adapted tool we specified before extraction; 30 of 63 sat at high risk, mostly because verification conduct was never reported
- One first version figure should not be quoted again: the major inaccuracy pool moved from 36.0% to 16.5% after settlement removed counts that did not match the printed definitions

The spread between setups was the real finding. In one radiology study, six models given the same task fabricated between 3% and 61% of 480 references each. A pooled average describes that literature. It does not describe any one product.

Everything is open: manuscript, protocol, search log, screening and extraction data (both passes), risk of bias assessments, analysis code and forest plots, CC BY 4.0.

Title: One in five does not exist: a systematic review and meta-analysis of fabricated citations in LLM generated biomedical reference lists

Background: Health sciences librarians increasingly field reference lists drafted by large language models. No pooled estimate of fabrication in generated biomedical references existed.
Objectives: To pool, from generation experiments, the proportions of generated references that are completely fabricated, that carry any major inaccuracy, and that are fully accurate.
Methods: Cochrane Handbook 6.5 methods in structure, PRISMA 2020 reporting, dual LLM screening with adjudication by reading under a single accountable human author, a second fully independent extraction with every discrepancy settled against the source, an adapted risk of bias tool specified before extraction, random effects meta-analysis of proportions (logit, REML), GRADE adapted for proportion evidence.
Results: 16,474 records screened; 63 experiments (148 model arms) included in the corrected version. Completely fabricated: pooled 19.4% (95% CI 14.9 to 24.8; 113 arms; I squared 97.1%). Fully accurate: 37.0% (27.2 to 47.9; 58 arms). Retrieval active arms pooled 5.8% fabricated against 31.0% with retrieval off (26 retrieval arms). All critical outcomes rated very low certainty, driven by unreported verification conduct and heterogeneity.
Conclusions: Generated reference lists need entry by entry verification against a database before use. The verification step is library work, and this literature rarely reports how it was done.
