# Cover letter: Journal of the Medical Library Association

Dear Editors,

I submit "Fabricated and inaccurate citations generated by large language models for biomedical topics: a systematic review and meta-analysis" for consideration as a Knowledge Synthesis article in the Journal of the Medical Library Association.

The question sits in your readers' daily work. Health sciences librarians verify reference lists, support systematic reviews and teach citation practice, and they now receive reference lists drafted by large language models at scale. Until this review, the evidence on how often those lists invent papers existed only as single studies that disagreed with each other by an order of magnitude. This review pooled 25 generation experiments and 69 model arms on their shared denominator, references generated and checked one by one against a named database. It found 18.6 percent of generated references completely fabricated and 29.4 percent fully accurate, with every critical outcome rated very low certainty and the reasons stated. A published literature audit is reported in a separate stratum and never pooled with the experiments.

Three features may matter to your reviewers. The risk of bias tool was adapted for citation generation experiments and specified before extraction; its main finding is that verification conduct is the least reported part of this literature, which is a finding about reporting practice in a domain your readers audit for a living. All data, code, screening files and assessments are published openly with the manuscript (CC BY 4.0), so any reader can rebuild every pooled figure. The manuscript follows PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-S, and the search log records blocked and unrun sources as blocked and unrun.

The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published. A preprint will be deposited on Zenodo and medRxiv at submission, and that deposit is declared here in advance. I have no conflicts of interest and received no funding. AI tools were used in the review process under my direction; that use is reported in full in the manuscript's declarations and in the published team log, in line with the journal's expectations on transparency.

The manuscript in its Cochrane order form runs longer than the journal's 5,000 word main text guide. A condensed cut to the journal's limit, keeping the abstract within 250 words and the tables and figures within six, is prepared in this package (manuscript-jmla-condensed.md) and is the version offered for peer review; the full version stands as the preprint and the repository record, and the condensed cut changes no number.
