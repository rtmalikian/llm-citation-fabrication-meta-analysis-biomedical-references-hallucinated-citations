# LLM team log

Review 2: fabricated and inaccurate citations generated by large language models for biomedical topics. All work dated 22 October 2026 (Pacific). Raphael Malikian is the accountable human author. Roles were filled by LLM agents working from the prompts in prompts.md, in the same arrangement as Review 1. Every number below comes from a file in this folder.

## Roles

Information specialist (search), screener A (strict), screener B (sensitive), adjudicator, extractors (three parallel batches plus a fourth batch for re-retrieved records), risk of bias assessors 1 and 2 working apart, adjudicator for risk of bias, statistician, GRADE assessor, methods editor.

## Search

Sources run, hit counts and retrieved counts sit in search-log.md. Totals: 16,497 records retrieved across all runs; 512 duplicates removed (DOI first, then normalised title); 15,985 records went to screening. arXiv and Semantic Scholar were attempted and blocked (arXiv timeout then HTTP 429; Semantic Scholar HTTP 429) and are logged as blocked, never as searched. OpenAlex returned a 25 record test page only; full retrieval was not run and the log says so. The Europe PMC string was not restricted to title and abstract, so its 14,090 retrieved records were deliberately broad and noisy. Crossref total-results figures (1,507,235 and 122,445) are relevance match counts, not result sets, and were never used as counts of records.

## Title and abstract screening (stage 1)

Screener A (strict) and screener B (sensitive) applied different written rule sets to all 15,985 titles and abstracts, working apart. Decisions were merged by code in screen_stage1.py.

- Screener A included 71 records. Screener B included 984.
- Both included 71, both excluded 15,001, B only 913, A only 0. Agreement: 15,072 of 15,985, 94.29 percent.
- Cohen kappa: 0.13. The low kappa with high raw agreement is the prevalence paradox: almost every record was an easy exclude for both screeners, so chance agreement was very high, and the 913 B-only records (sensitive pass picking up records whose titles carry no citation term) drive the statistic down. The disagreement breakdown is reported rather than smoothed over.
- Adjudicator rule, applied by code: a B-only record passed only if the title carried a citation or reference term and the abstract carried a generation or audit signal. Adjudicated includes: 122. Excluded at this stage: 15,863.

## Full text stage

Full texts were sought for all 122 adjudicated records (automated open access retrieval for every survivor, Europe PMC XML first, then Unpaywall). This differs from Review 1, where a strict abstract pass cut the list before retrieval. The amendment and its reason (retrieval was automated and cheap; the strict pass was applied at full text instead) are logged in protocol.md under differences between protocol and review.

- Retrieved: 76 (49 Europe PMC XML, 22 Unpaywall PDF, 5 Unpaywall HTML). Not retrieved: 46, listed in flagged-for-author.md. No paywall workarounds were used.
- Retrieval integrity problem, found at extraction and reported here in full: the PMCID field in the deduplicated search set was wrong for a number of records, so a retrieved file sometimes contained a different article than its record. Extractors flagged every mismatch in the screening files. Eleven records went to re-retrieval by DOI and exact title through the Europe PMC search API, with the article title inside the returned XML checked against the record title. Six correct full texts were recovered (R0043, R0045, R0053, R0180, R0263, R0364) and re-extracted in batch 4. Four records were not found (R0030, R0050, R0065, R0100) and one had no PMCID (R0111). Three of the mismatched records had already been resolved as duplicates or as the same experiment captured under another record. Four reports (R0030, R0050, R0065, R0111) could not be assessed on their intended text and were excluded for that stated reason; they are counted inside the 48 full text exclusions and named in flagged-for-author.md.
- Full text screening of the 76 assessed reports: 48 excluded with one reason each (fulltext-screening.csv), 27 reports of generation experiments included, 1 published literature audit included as the separate stratum.
- Duplicate reports merged at study level, never double pooled: Aljamaan 2024 (R5083 retained, R0115 duplicate extraction dropped), Tas 2026 (R3715 retained, R0100 dropped), Athaluri 2023 (R0094 retained, R0364 dropped), plus duplicate reports excluded at full text (Gravel preprint, Dhaimade preprint, Mugaanyi duplicate, Walters and Wilder duplicate, MIH duplicate). Dedup log: stage4/dedup-log.md.
- Unique studies included: 25 generation experiments (69 model arms extracted) and 1 audit (Yin 2026).

## Extraction

Dual extraction in this review means: first extraction by the batch extractors from full text, then a verification pass. The verification pass hand checked pooled totals against the extraction sheet and checked individual arms against printed values in their papers:

- Analysis totals check: fabricated 2,324 events in 12,828 references (crude 0.1812), major inaccuracy 522 in 1,537 (0.3396), fully accurate 5,239 in 12,002 (0.4365). The analysis script's crude figures match these hand sums exactly (forest_plots.py output).
- Chelli 2024, GPT-3.5 arm: 55 of 139 fabricated, 39.6 percent, matches the value printed in the paper.
- Bernstein 2026: 195 of 2,736 fabricated, 7.13 percent, matches the abstract and Appendix table sums (per guideline splits sum to the totals).
- Seifi 2026: per model fabricated counts derived from printed percentages at a printed denominator of 100 per model sum to the printed pooled 85 of 300. Derivation flagged in every affected row.
- Gunes 2026: six arms of 480 references each, fabricated counts 15, 88, 133, 254, 226, 291 as printed in its Table 2.

Extraction discrepancies kept visible: R2250 (per model hallucinated counts not printed, left blank, no back calculation), Metze 2024 (percentages only, counts blank), Dhaimade 2026 arm labelling inconsistency between text and table, Sawamura 2024 denominator 37 in text against 38 in table sections, Shi 2026 printed rates that do not all match its printed counts. Each sits in the definitions_note of its rows.

## Risk of bias

Two assessors applied the adapted tool in rob-forms.md to all 25 generation studies, working apart, 8 domains each, 200 domain judgements. Scale wording differed between the two files (assessor 1 used low, some concerns, high, no information; assessor 2 used low, moderate, high, unclear); the files were normalised to the rob-forms.md wording before adjudication (moderate equals some concerns, unclear equals no information), and the normalisation is stated here and in rob-summary.md.

- Domain disagreements: 56 of 200. By domain: D1 11, D2 11, D8 11, D7 8, D3 6, D6 4, D4 3, D5 2.
- Overall disagreements: 5 of 25.
- Adjudication rule applied by code (rob_adjudicate.py): for each domain and for the overall judgement, the more cautious of the two judgements was taken. This rule is conservative and is stated in the manuscript.
- Adjudicated overall: high risk 15 studies, some concerns 10, low 0.

## Analysis

Statistician role: analysis.py, Python 3 with numpy, scipy and pandas (versions in the code header output), random effects meta-analysis of proportions, logit transformation, REML tau squared, prediction interval where 10 or more arms pool. R was not available in this environment; the protocol named Python as the stated fallback. All pooled figures in the manuscript come from stage4/analysis-results.json and can be reproduced by running analysis.py on extraction.csv.

## GRADE

GRADE assessor applied the adapted GRADE approach for proportion evidence set out in protocol.md. All three critical outcomes were rated very low certainty. Reasons per outcome sit in the summary of findings table in the manuscript.

## Correction log, 22 October 2026 (evening): forest plots and PDF build

Author flagged that the summary diamond was not visible in the PDF forest plots. Checked against the files, the fault was in the build, in three parts. (1) forest_plots.py (v1) never drew a summary diamond at all: it drew only a dashed vertical line at the pooled value. (2) build_docx.py appended the v1 images at a fixed 6.5in width; the fabricated image (aspect 1.63) then stood 10.6in tall, taller than the page, so the converted PDF pushed it onto its own page, orphaned its heading on a blank page, and clipped the bottom of the plot including the axis label. (3) build_docx.py never appended forest-major_inaccuracy.png at all, so that plot was missing from the PDF and DOCX entirely. The pooled numbers were not affected: they come from analysis.py and stage4/analysis-results.json, not from the plots.

Fix: forest_plots_v2.py draws by-study plots (one row per study) and arm-level panels of at most 22 arms, each with the pooled proportion as a red diamond (tips at the 95% CI), the prediction interval as a capped line where available, weight-sized squares, events/total and percent per row, and a fixed 0 to 100% axis with its label inside the figure. build_docx.py now appends Figures 1 to 3 (by study, all three outcomes, major inaccuracy restored), the six arm panels as appendix figures, and the PRISMA diagram, scaling every image to fit the page and keeping each heading with its figure. DOCX and PDF rebuilt (25 pages) and every figure page was rendered and inspected: diamonds visible, no clipping, no orphaned headings. The v1 plot files remain in the folder and repository for the record.
