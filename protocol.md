# Protocol

## Fabricated citations from large language models: how often references are completely fabricated, how often they contain a major inaccuracy, and how often they are fully accurate

### A systematic review and meta-analysis of citation generation experiments, with a separate stratum for audits of the published literature

Author: Raphael Malikian. Healthcare founder and operator, clinical AI builder. Independent researcher, no industry affiliation. ORCID: 0009-0008-4326-4205.

Protocol version: 1.0, 22 October 2026.
Registration: prepared for PROSPERO and for the Open Science Framework (OSF). No PROSPERO registration number exists at the time of writing. No PROSPERO number was issued before analysis. This protocol will not be backdated. The review will report the date a registration number is issued, if one is issued, and will state plainly if registration remains pending at publication.
Methods standard: Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, version 6.5 (2024), applied in spirit and structure to a review of proportions. Reporting will follow PRISMA 2020. The search will be reported to PRISMA-S. This review is Cochrane level in method. It is not a Cochrane review and will not be submitted to Cochrane.
Scope note: this is evidence synthesis and research integrity discussion only. It makes no clinical recommendation and no medication recommendation, and it gives no advice for the care of any individual patient.

## 1. Background

Large language models invent references. A generated reference can look complete. It carries author names that sound right, a journal name that exists, a year that fits the topic, a volume and pages, and sometimes a DOI. Verification often shows that no such publication exists. In other cases a real publication sits behind part of the reference, but the reference as printed cannot be used to find it, or it points to a different paper.

Primary studies have tested this by asking models to generate references for biomedical and clinical topics and then checking each reference against PubMed, Crossref, Google Scholar, a publisher record or another named source. Reported rates differ widely across studies. The differences track the model, whether the model could search or retrieve, the topic, and how many references the study asked for. A study that asks for five references on a well known condition and a study that asks for thirty references on a narrow recent topic are not measuring the same task, even though both report a share fabricated.

A second kind of study answers a different question. Large audits scan the published literature or preprint servers and estimate how many published papers or published references show signs of fabrication. One audit in 2026 covered 2.5 million biomedical papers. Audits use a published paper or a published reference as the denominator. Generation experiments use a generated reference as the denominator. The two denominators answer different questions and the review keeps them apart at every stage.

Earlier reviews of hallucination cover many tasks at once. Summarisation, question answering and citation generation do not share a denominator, so a single pooled hallucination rate across those tasks means little. Citation generation does share a denominator in nearly every primary study: references generated. That shared denominator is why this review takes citation fabrication as its question and leaves broader hallucination rates to narrative work.

No published synthesis pools the generation experiments on a common set of definitions, with model arms kept visible and with subgroups set in advance for model generation, retrieval, topic type and list length. That gap is the reason for this review.

## Purpose

This review asks what happens when a large language model is asked for biomedical references. It asks what share of the references it returns do not exist at all, what share contain an error that stops a reader finding the source or that credits the wrong source, and what share are right on every field checked. It asks how those shares shift with the model, with retrieval or browsing, with the topic, and with the length of the list the study requested.

Research questions:
- RQ1. What proportion of references generated by large language models for biomedical and clinical topics are completely fabricated, where no matching publication exists?
- RQ2. What proportion contain a major inaccuracy, including completely fabricated references, where major means the error prevents retrieval or misattributes the source?
- RQ3. What proportion are fully accurate on all fields checked?
- RQ4. What are the rates of minor errors by field, and what proportion carry a DOI that does not resolve or that resolves to a different paper?
- RQ5. Does the error rate change by position in the generated list?
- RQ6. How do the proportions in RQ1 to RQ3 vary by model family and generation, by use of retrieval, browsing or a reference database, by specialty or topic, by established against emerging topic, by number of references requested, by peer reviewed against preprint status, and by model year?
- RQ7. What do audits of the published literature report, as a separate stratum, about fabricated citations that reached print?

## 2. Objectives

To estimate, from citation generation experiments, the proportions of large language model generated biomedical references that are completely fabricated, that contain a major inaccuracy, and that are fully accurate, and to examine how those proportions vary by model, retrieval, topic and list length, with audits of the published literature reported as a separate stratum.

## 3. Eligibility criteria

### 3.1 Types of studies: generation experiments, main synthesis

Include:
- Primary studies that prompted one or more large language models to generate references or citations on a defined biomedical, clinical or health topic.
- Studies that report the number of references generated, so a denominator in generated references can be extracted.
- Studies that verified each generated reference against a named source. Acceptable sources include PubMed, Crossref, Google Scholar, a publisher record, a journal record, a library catalogue, or an equivalent check named in the study. The source must be external to the model that generated the references.
- Studies that report, or allow extraction of, a count of completely fabricated references, a count with major inaccuracy, or a count fully accurate, with the denominator for that count.
- Peer reviewed journal articles and preprints with original data. Conference papers with original data and a full methods report are included on the same test.

### 3.2 Separate stratum: audits of the published literature

Include in the audit stratum, and never pool with generation experiments:
- Large scale audits of the published literature, of journal corpora, or of preprint servers that estimate how many published papers contain fabricated references, or how many published references are fabricated.
- Audits that state their corpus, their detection method, and their denominator in papers or in published references.

Audit studies are extracted on the audit section of the extraction form, tabulated apart, and discussed apart. They show where generation errors end up. They do not enter any pooled proportion in the main synthesis.

### 3.3 Exclusions

Exclude from both the main synthesis and the audit stratum:
- Editorials, commentaries, reviews, and opinion pieces with no original generation experiment and no original audit. These are used for background and reference chasing only.
- Single reference anecdotes with no defined denominator.
- Studies of hallucination in clinical text, summarisation or question answering with no citation outcome.
- Studies where verification was against the model own output, with no external source named.
- Studies that report only a mean accuracy score with no count and no denominator that can be extracted.
- Vendor marketing material, product pages, and blog posts with no methods report.
- Duplicate reports of the same experiment. Where a preprint and a journal article report the same generation run, the journal article is the main record and the preprint is linked to it. Where two reports cover overlapping runs and the overlap cannot be separated, both are extracted, the overlap is flagged, and a sensitivity check uses one report only.

### 3.4 Models and generation task

Models: any large language model named in the study, with model name and version extracted as printed. Studies that test several models contribute one arm per model where the study reports results per model. Studies that pool models in print with no per model split contribute a single mixed arm, flagged as mixed.

Task: the model was asked to produce references, a reference list, citations for a passage, or references in support of stated claims, on a biomedical, clinical or health topic. Studies where the model was given real references and asked only to format them are excluded, since no generation of source identity took place. Studies where the model was given a topic and also given access to retrieval, browsing, a search tool, a reference database, or a document set are included, with the retrieval condition extracted as an arm level field. Retrieval is a subgroup variable. It is not an exclusion.

Language of the prompt and of the topic is extracted where stated. No model, vendor or country is excluded.

### 3.5 Topics

Include topics that are biomedical, clinical or health related, as defined by the study. Extract specialty or topic type as printed, the review specialty grouping set in the extraction form, and whether the topic is established or emerging under the rule in section 3.6. Studies that mix biomedical topics with topics from other fields are included if the biomedical results are reported apart. If the split is not reported, the study is included as a mixed topic study and flagged, and it enters specialty subgroups only as mixed.

### 3.6 Established against emerging topics

The distinction is applied at extraction from what the study states and from the topic itself, under a fixed rule, and the basis for each call is logged:
- Established: a topic with a settled literature that predates the model training period in the main, for example a long standing condition, procedure or drug class with standard references in wide use.
- Emerging: a topic where the literature is recent, fast moving, narrow, or centred on publications from the last three years before the generation date, for example a new pathogen topic, a recent guideline area, or a niche technique with few standard references.
Where the study itself labels topics in a comparable way, the study label is extracted as printed and the review call is set beside it. Where the call cannot be made from the paper, the field is coded as unclear. Unclear topics do not enter the established against emerging subgroup.

### 3.7 Report characteristics

Dates: no start date limit at search. Citation generation studies track the release of the models, and early work from 2022 onward may be eligible. The search end date is the date the search is run, and the run date is logged per source. Language: no limit at search. Non-English reports will be translated where a translation can be checked, and otherwise listed as awaiting classification. They will not be dropped without a record. Publication type: peer reviewed journal articles, preprints with original data, and conference papers with a full methods report, as in section 3.1.

## 4. Outcome measures

Definitions in this section are fixed before screening ends. The extraction form repeats them word for word. Where a primary study uses a different definition, its numbers are extracted as printed, mapped only where the mapping is exact, and otherwise kept in the narrative synthesis. The mapping for each study is logged. No study is forced into a pool its definitions do not fit.

### 4.1 Critical outcomes, generation experiments

Denominator for outcomes 1 to 3: references generated and verified in that study arm, as reported. Where a study generated references it did not verify, both counts are extracted and the verified count is the denominator, with the gap noted.

1. Completely fabricated. Proportion of generated references for which no matching publication exists. A reference counts as completely fabricated when verification against the named source finds no publication that matches on title and source identity in a way that could count as the same work. A reference that takes a real title and attaches wrong authors, or takes real authors and attaches a title that does not exist, counts here if no single matching publication exists.
2. Major inaccuracy. Proportion of generated references with any major inaccuracy, including completely fabricated references. A major inaccuracy is an error that prevents retrieval of the intended source, or that misattributes the source. It includes: a completely fabricated reference; a fabricated element that forms part of the identity of the source, such as a fabricated title, fabricated first author, or fabricated DOI; a journal, year, volume or page set that points to a different publication or to no publication; and a real publication cited for a claim or topic it does not cover where the study counts that as misattribution. Minor errors that leave the source findable and correctly credited do not count here.
3. Fully accurate. Proportion of generated references that are accurate on all fields checked by the study. All checked fields must be correct for a reference to count here: authors, title, journal or source, year, and volume, pages and DOI where the study checked them. A reference with any error in a checked field, major or minor, does not count as fully accurate under the strict definition. The lenient definition, used only in a sensitivity analysis, counts a reference as accurate if the source is real, correctly credited, and retrievable despite a minor field error. Each study is coded for which definition its printed accurate count matches, strict, lenient, or neither.

### 4.2 Important outcomes, generation experiments

4. Minor errors by field. Proportion of generated references with an error in each field, reported field by field and never combined into a single minor error pool unless the study reports a combined count on a stated definition: authors, title, journal or source, year, volume or pages, DOI. An error counts as minor when the source remains the same publication, remains retrievable from the rest of the reference, and remains correctly credited in the main.
5. Bad DOI. Proportion of generated references that carry a DOI, where the DOI does not resolve, or resolves to a different paper. The denominator for the resolves to a different paper rate is references that carry a DOI. The denominator for the does not resolve rate is the same, and both denominators are extracted as printed counts.
6. Error by position in the list. Error rate for early references against late references in the same generated list, where the study reports position. The split the study uses is extracted as printed. Where raw per position counts allow a common split, first half against second half, that split is derived and logged as derived. Position results are reported per study and pooled only where splits match.
7. Verification method and verifier agreement. Verification source and method per study, time or steps per reference where reported, and agreement between verifiers where a study used two or more verifiers, extracted as reported.

### 4.3 Audit stratum outcomes

For audits, reported apart and never pooled with section 4.1:
- Proportion of published papers in the corpus that contain at least one fabricated reference, where the audit reports it.
- Proportion of published references in the corpus that are fabricated, where the audit reports it.
- Corpus, years covered, detection method, and validation of the detection method, as printed.

Outcomes with no data are reported as no data in the summary of findings table or the audit table. They are not removed from the list because the result was empty.

## 5. Search methods

Sources to be searched, each run dated with string and hit count logged in the search log: PubMed (MEDLINE through NCBI E-utilities), Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Crossref, and arXiv and medRxiv for preprints, through the routes available at run time. Every retrieved record will be exported with source named per row.

Sources prepared but not run until access exists: Embase, Scopus and Web of Science. Exact ready to run strings for each sit in search-appendix-pending.md, marked string prepared, run pending access. The review will not state that those databases were searched until a dated run with a hit count is added to the search log. A source that is blocked or rate limited at run time is logged as blocked, with the date and the error, never as searched.

Search blocks: model terms, citation and reference terms, and fabrication and accuracy terms. The search was built for generation experiments first. A second block set hunts audits, with corpus and published literature terms, so audit studies are found by design and not by chance. Full strings as run will sit in the search log. Any change to a string after this protocol will be logged as an amendment with a date and a reason.

Citation work planned after full text retrieval: reference lists of all included studies and of existing reviews of language model hallucination, and forward citation chasing on the earliest systematic generation experiments identified at screening.

Grey and supplementary routes: preprint servers as above. No vendor sites will be searched as a source of studies. Conference proceedings are found through the databases above and through reference chasing.

## 6. Selection of studies

Two stages: title and abstract, then full text. Each stage is recorded in counts that feed the PRISMA 2020 flow: records identified per source, duplicates removed, records screened, records excluded at title and abstract, full texts sought, full texts not retrieved, full text exclusions with one reason each, studies included in the main synthesis, and studies included in the audit stratum.

At full text, each study is assigned to one of three calls: include in generation experiments, include in audit stratum, or exclude with one reason. A study that contains both a generation experiment and an audit is split at extraction, with each part assigned to its stratum and the split logged.

### LLM team in place of a human research team

A Cochrane review is normally written by a team: a search specialist, two screeners who work apart, two extractors, a statistician, and editors who check method. This review has one human author. In place of that team it uses a team of large language model roles, set up to work the way the human team works, with the same separations. The point is to come as close to a Cochrane review as one author can get, and to report, in numbers, how well the substitute team held up.

Roles, named to match the human jobs. Exact prompts sit in prompts.md:

1. Information specialist. Builds a search string for each database in that database syntax, runs it or marks it run pending access, and records string, date and hit count in the search log. No database is reported as searched without a dated run.
2. Screener A. Works through every title and abstract alone and records include, exclude or maybe, with a reason for each exclude. Screener A works to the strict prompt.
3. Screener B. Does the same work in a separate pass, with the sensitive prompt, and does not see screener A calls until both passes are complete.
4. Adjudicator. A third pass. Takes every record where A and B disagree, reads title and abstract again, and settles the call with a logged reason. Items it cannot settle are flagged for the human author, by record ID.
5. Extractor A. Extracts each included study on the piloted form in extraction-form.md.
6. Extractor B. Extracts the same studies alone, in a separate pass. The two sheets are compared field by field. Every mismatch is logged and settled against the source document.
7. Risk of bias assessor pair. Two independent passes with the adapted tool in rob-forms.md, per study arm where the tool applies per arm, with domain judgements and the text that backs each one. Disagreements go to the human author.
8. Statistician. Writes the analysis code, runs it, and checks it a second way: a hand check of one pool against the extraction sheet, and a rerun after any correction. Code and data are posted with the review.
9. GRADE assessor. Drafts the GRADE rating per critical outcome with the reason for each downgrade or the reason none was made, against the points set in section 10. The human author signs each rating.
10. Methods editor. Checks the finished manuscript against PRISMA 2020, PRISMA-S, MECIR style conduct standards, and the Cochrane Handbook structure. Its checklist is filed with the manuscript.

Independence rules:
- A and B passes are run apart. Neither prompt contains the other pass calls, and the files are written apart before any comparison.
- Comparison happens only in a merge step, by code, which counts agreements and disagreements. The merge step makes no judgement of its own.
- The adjudicator sees both calls and both reasons, plus the record. It does not see which pass the human author prefers, since he has not recorded one.
- Prompts, model name and version, and dates for each role sit in the team log. A prompt changed mid-task is a new version in the log, and work done under the old version is named.

Human oversight:
Raphael Malikian is principal investigator and accountable human author. He set the review question and the eligibility rules in this protocol. He settles every item the adjudicator flags, every risk of bias disagreement, and every GRADE rating, and he checks every extracted number that enters a pool against the source document before he signs off the final numbers. No number in the finished review rests on a model pass alone.

Disclosure, RAISE 2025:
This use follows the 2025 RAISE statement on responsible AI use in evidence synthesis, from Cochrane, Campbell, JBI and the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence. Tool, version, dates, purpose and checks are reported here and in the finished review. Any journal or conference AI use statement will carry the same facts: a team of language model roles did the team tasks, under the separations above, and the named human author is accountable for every judgement.

Mechanics reported as results:
The workings of the team are data, and the finished review reports them in its methods and results: inter-screener agreement rate and Cohen kappa at title and abstract, the count and type of screener disagreements, adjudication outcomes split into A upheld, B upheld, and sent to the author, full text agreement in the same form, and the extraction discrepancy rate per field. The log that holds these counts is the team log. Reporting them is part of the contribution of this review: a reader can judge the substitute team on its numbers, the same way they judge the primary studies.

## 7. Data extraction

Two independent extractor passes, A and B, as set out in the LLM team section, using the piloted form in extraction-form.md. The sheets are compared field by field by code. Each mismatch is logged with its field name, settled against the source document, and counted toward the extraction discrepancy rate in the team log. The human author checks every number that will enter a pool. The form was drafted from the outcomes in section 4 and the bias concerns in section 8 before screening ends.

Items extracted for generation experiments: study identity; country; specialty or topic; established against emerging call with its basis; each model and model version as printed; generation date and generation settings as printed; retrieval, browsing or reference database condition; prompt as printed or summarised with a verbatim copy where the study gives one; references requested and references generated; verification source and verification method; verifier independence and blinding; outcome definitions as printed; counts for each outcome with denominators, per arm where reported; funding source and conflicts as stated. The full item list, with field numbers, sits in extraction-form.md.

Items for the audit stratum sit in a separate section of the same form: corpus, years, denominator, detection method, validation, and counts as printed. Audit fields are never merged into generation experiment fields.

Study authors will be contacted for missing counts or denominators where an address is given, once, with a two week window. Replies and non-replies are logged. A count supplied by an author is marked as supplied, with the date.

## 8. Risk of bias

No standard tool fits citation generation experiments cleanly. RoB 2 covers randomised trials of interventions. QUADAS-2 covers diagnostic accuracy studies. A generation experiment is neither. The protocol therefore pre-specifies an adapted tool, filed as rob-forms.md before extraction begins. The tool is reported as adapted at every mention. It is never presented as RoB 2 or as QUADAS-2, and its judgements are never merged with judgements from those tools.

Summary of the adapted tool. Domains:
1. Topic and prompt sampling. How topics and prompts were chosen, how many were used, and whether the choice favours easy or hard topics.
2. Prompt and model version reporting. Whether the prompt, the model name, the exact version or release, and the generation date are reported well enough for another team to repeat the run.
3. Generation settings. Whether settings that change output, such as temperature, browsing on or off, system instructions, retries, and selection among multiple outputs, are reported and held fixed across arms.
4. Verification source. Whether verification used a named external source fit for the field, and whether the check covered every element the outcomes rest on.
5. Verifier independence. Whether the people or tools that verified references were independent of the generation step, and whether vendor staff took part in verification.
6. Duplicate or blinded verification. Whether references were checked twice, or by a verifier who did not know which model produced them, and how disagreements between verifiers were settled.
7. Handling of partial matches. Whether the study set rules in advance for near matches, for changed titles, for preprints that later appeared in journals, and for references that mix real and fabricated elements, and whether those rules match the review definitions or diverge from them.
8. Selective reporting. Whether all models, topics and prompts the study ran are reported with counts, or whether reporting picks favourable arms, favourable topics, or favourable thresholds.

Each domain is judged low, some concerns, or high, with no information used where the paper gives too little to judge. Each judgement needs a support line: a quote or a page and table reference from the paper. A judgement with no support line does not count. Judgements are made per study, and per arm where a domain varies by arm, for example where one arm had retrieval and another did not and generation settings are reported per arm only. An overall judgement per study follows the rule in rob-forms.md.

Two independent assessor passes make each judgement, as set out in the LLM team section. Agreements stand. Disagreements, with domain named, go to the human author for settlement, and the count is logged in the team log.

For audit stratum studies, the same form is used with the audit notes in rob-forms.md. Sampling in that stratum covers corpus sampling. Verification covers validation of the detection method against hand checked references.

## 9. Synthesis

### 9.1 Main analysis

Analysis in R with the meta or metafor packages. Where R is unavailable in the analysis environment, analysis in Python with an equivalent random effects model for proportions will be used, and the finished review will state which software ran the final numbers, with package and version. The fallback is stated here in advance so a software switch is not a post hoc change. Code written and checked by the statistician role, and signed off by the human author, will be posted with the review either way.

Model: random effects meta-analysis of proportions. Logit transformation as the primary transformation. Tau squared estimated by restricted maximum likelihood, with the estimator named in the code and the report where it differs. I squared and tau squared reported for every pool. A prediction interval is reported where ten or more studies or model arms pool. Confidence intervals for single proportions use the method the chosen package applies to logit transformed proportions, back transformed for reporting, and the method is named in the report.

Unit of analysis: the model arm. A study that tests three models contributes three arms to the per arm dataset, each with its own numerator and denominator. Study is kept as a clustering level. Where the data allow, the primary pool uses a model that accounts for arms nested in studies. Where that model cannot be fitted on the data in hand, the per arm pool is run with arms treated as units and the limitation is stated in the results. In either case a per study sensitivity pool is always shown, where each study contributes one combined proportion, so a reader can see whether one study with many models drives the result. The two pools are labelled per arm and per study at every mention.

Three critical outcomes are pooled apart: completely fabricated, major inaccuracy, fully accurate. A reference can sit in more than one of these counts by design, since major inaccuracy includes completely fabricated. The pools are therefore reported side by side and never added together. Minor error fields, bad DOI rates and position results are pooled only where definitions and denominators match across studies, and otherwise reported per study in tables.

Where pooling would mix definitions that do not map, no pool is run for that outcome and study set. The result is reported with SWiM style structured narrative synthesis, with the proportion, denominator, model and risk of bias shown per study.

Audit stratum studies are never pooled with generation experiments. Audits are pooled with each other only if at least three audits share a denominator type, a comparable corpus type and a comparable detection method. In that case the audit pool is labelled as an audit pool at every mention, with its denominator stated. Otherwise audits are tabulated and described.

### 9.2 Subgroup analyses, set in advance

Subgroups are limited to the list below, fixed in the concept document and in this protocol. No other subgroup will be presented as pre-specified. Any subgroup added after seeing results will be labelled post hoc in the finished review.

- Model family and generation. Arms grouped by family as printed, for example GPT family by generation, Claude family, Gemini family, Llama family, and other families with at least two arms, with single arm families grouped as other. Generation follows the study report and the model release record.
- Retrieval against none. Arms with retrieval, browsing, search tool use or a reference database against arms with no retrieval aid. Arms where the retrieval condition is unclear do not enter this subgroup.
- Specialty or topic type. Grouped by the review specialty grouping on the extraction form. Mixed topic studies form their own group.
- Established against emerging topic. As defined in section 3.6. Arms coded unclear do not enter this subgroup.
- List length. References requested per prompt or per list: 10 or fewer against more than 10. Where a study varies list length within an arm, the arm enters only if a split by length is reported.
- Peer reviewed against preprint. By report type of the study, not of the references generated.
- Model year. 2023 or earlier against 2024 against 2025 onward. Model year is the release year of the model version tested, taken from the study report or the vendor release record, and the source for each arm is logged. Where release year cannot be set, the arm is coded unclear and does not enter this subgroup.

Subgroup claims rest on a test for interaction or an equivalent between group heterogeneity test from the fitted model. A result inside one subgroup reaching a threshold on its own is not a subgroup finding. Subgroups with fewer than two arms in a group are described, not tested.

### 9.3 Sensitivity analyses, set in advance

- Definition strict against lenient for fully accurate. Main pool uses the strict definition in section 4.1. Sensitivity pool uses the lenient definition where studies report counts that fit it.
- Low or moderate risk of bias only. Studies judged overall low or some concerns or moderate equivalent under the adapted tool wording in rob-forms.md. The finished review will use one wording set from rob-forms.md and apply it in both places.
- Leave one model family out, run in turn for each family with at least two arms.
- Preprints removed.
- Per study pool, as set out in section 9.1, shown for every critical outcome pool.
- Mapping sensitivity. Pools rerun with studies whose outcome definitions mapped with any doubt removed, leaving only studies whose printed definitions match the review definitions word for word in substance.

### 9.4 Reporting bias

Funnel plot and a test for small study effects only where ten or more units pool, and interpreted with care. For proportion meta-analyses funnel asymmetry has causes other than reporting bias, including true differences between small studies on narrow topics and large studies on broad topics. The review will discuss any asymmetry as a pattern to explain, and will not present a funnel test as proof of reporting bias on its own. Selective reporting judgements from domain 8 of the adapted tool are reported beside any funnel result.

### 9.5 Heterogeneity

Heterogeneity is expected. Model, topic and list length all change the task. A high I squared is a finding to explain through the subgroups above, not a reason to hide the pool. Where heterogeneity stays high and unexplained, the prediction interval carries the message and the summary of findings table says so.

## 10. Certainty of the evidence

GRADE adapted for proportion and prevalence evidence, applied per critical outcome, for a summary of findings table covering the three critical outcomes. The starting point and each downgrade are stated in the report. No upgrading is expected for any outcome.

Downgrade reasons set in advance:
- Risk of bias. Downgrade where pooled arms rest heavily on studies with high risk of bias in verification domains, in particular verification source, verifier independence and duplicate verification, as judged with the adapted tool.
- Inconsistency. Downgrade where heterogeneity is high and the pre-specified subgroups do not explain it, or where the prediction interval covers values that would lead a reader to opposite conclusions about how common fabrication is.
- Indirectness. Downgrade where generated lists in experiments stand in for real manuscript drafting. Experiments set the topic, set the list length and often ask for references with no surrounding text to support. Real drafting gives a model claims, context and sometimes a document set. The gap between the experiment task and the drafting task is the indirectness concern, and it is stated in those terms.
- Imprecision. Downgrade where the pooled confidence interval is wide enough to cover rates that differ in practical meaning, or where the total number of references pooled is small for the outcome.
- Publication bias. Downgrade only where a reporting bias pattern under section 9.4 and selective reporting judgements together support it. A funnel test alone does not trigger a downgrade.

The GRADE assessor drafts each rating. The human author signs each rating. Footnotes in the summary of findings table give the reason for every downgrade in one sentence each, tied to the counts in the results.
